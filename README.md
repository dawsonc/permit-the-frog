<p align="center"><em>Understanding the cost of home electrification using permit data.</em></p>

Electrifying your home is one of the few areas where individuals can have a direct impact on climate change. Getting policy and systemic change right is important, but home electrification is fundamentally a kitchen-table issue. While switching your home from fossil fuels to electric heating is cleaner, more comfortable, and often makes your house cheaper to heat in the long run, the up-front cost can be eye-watering.  <!-- In many states, subsidies help homeowners and landlords install heat pumps, but not all of this subsidy goes to consumers (installers and manufacturers capture a share of it as higher prices). -->

Compounding this challenge is the *information asymmetry* between consumers and professional installers. While there are [groups](https://laminarcollective.com/) doing great work to bring transparency to this space, there's a lack of hard data. Fortunately, some cities and towns require contractors to share a project's cost when they file for a permit, making those data part of the public record in some places.

This repository scrapes permit cost data from Somerville (more to come) and presents it as a searchable webpage, grouping free-text project descriptions into categories like heat pump, panel upgrade, and solar.

*Note: while the address for each permit is part of the public record, I don't want to make it __easier__ to find anyone's personal information on the web, so this site presents permit data at a suitably anonymized level.*
